General expression — Level 1 (Eq. 1)
MP = (MI × NP) × SW × MSγ × CPα
MI — Maritime Interests NP — Naval Power SW — Strategic Will MS — Maritime Security CP — Cyber Power γ — weight of the security environment · α — weight of cyber dependence
Normalized formula — Eqs. (2)–(3)
MPnorm = [ MP ]1/neff  ·  neff = 3 + γ + α
weighted geometric mean: ωMI = ωNP = ωSW = 1/neff, ωMS = γ/neff, ωCP = α/neff · MPnorm ∈ (0, 1]
Proposition 1: MP_norm = G^(1−w) · CP^w, with G the geometric mean of the rest of the system and w = α/n_eff UNDP (2010); OECD–JRC (2008)

Responsiveness, marginal return and the cost condition (Table 5 · Corollary 1)
ComponentValueElasticity ωkΔMPnorm at +20 %∂MPnorm/∂XkBreak-even ck/cCP
Under normalization the elasticity of MPnorm with respect to each component equals its normalized weight ωk, so a 20 % increase changes the index by 1.2ωk − 1. Sensitivity measures responsiveness, not investment efficiency. The break-even ratio is the relative unit cost ck/cCP above which raising cyber power is more efficient than raising component k (Corollary 1, valid for small investments); the model contains no cost data, so it is a testable condition rather than a result.

The scale divides the range of the normalized formula into five equal intervals; its limits are conventions of the article, not thresholds estimated from data. Cyber maturity is read on CP itself, in five bands: incipient (0.00–0.20), basic (0.21–0.40), consolidating (0.41–0.60), advanced (0.61–0.80) and consolidated (0.81–1.00). α is a weight, not a maturity index: raising CP always raises MPnorm, whereas raising α raises it only if CP exceeds G, the geometric mean of the rest of the system, and lowers it otherwise (Proposition 1). MPnorm = [(MI × NP) × SW × MSγ × CPα]1/neff, neff = 3 + γ + α.
